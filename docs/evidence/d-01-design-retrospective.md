# From functioning controls to a usable workflow

## Outcome and evidence

After D-01B v4, the owner said: “alright, this is finally useable. still hideous, but at least fairly clear. make sure we're updating our strategy/objectives/docs with any post hoc analysis from this whole process.” This is qualified acceptance of overall usability and clarity, and negative feedback on visual quality. It is not a narrated test of every scenario, approval of authorization scope, or confirmation that review evidence is sufficient.

Evidence trail: [v1–v4 feedback and checks](d-01b-prototype-review.md), [v3 structural findings](../design/portal-foundation/feedback-2026-09-19.md), [R-07C failure audit](r-07c-process-validation.md), [D-01D live assessment](../design/portal-foundation/v1/assessment.md), [v4 work record](../design/process/d-01b-v4-work-record.md).

## What the sequence taught us

| Observation | Interpretation and durable response | Limit / next evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier prototypes passed behavior checks but the owner rejected their organization | Technical correctness is insufficient for product progress. Define the journey, page responsibility and transitions before composing a cross-page flow; check the actual accepted scope | Tests remain necessary for behavior; do not replace them with subjective approval |
| Adding workflow documents did not prevent v3 from extending Overview approval to surrounding pages | The failure was partly enforcement and evidence adequacy, not simply missing instructions. Use a scoped readiness verdict at the construction boundary | More files are not a success measure; inspect whether a later independent task follows the gate |
| D-01D exposed five structural gaps, selected a shell, and v4 then received the first positive usability feedback | This sequence supports continuing structure-first development with explicit collection/detail and state-action contracts | Association, not causal proof: fixture clarity, repeated feedback and accumulated familiarity also changed |
| A separate BorrowBox app clarified what was being built versus what managed development | Keep realistic independent-product fixtures and external review controls; exercise zero/one/many affected records | A fictional fixture does not establish actual second-product portability |
| Owner finds v4 usable but hideous | Structural clarity, interaction behavior and visual quality need separate acceptance. Preserve the working flow while exploring coherent visual alternatives | Visual work remains outstanding, not waived by low-fidelity intent |
| Several resets required direct owner intervention | Evaluate process by avoided rework and quality of reviewable outcomes, not document quantity or speed of first code | No reliable time/cost baseline exists; do not claim measured efficiency gains |

## Changes to strategy and objectives

The first valuable milestone remains a real owner-authorized change and agent-built preview. This episode does not justify a larger workflow engine or more product scope before proving that loop.

Use staged evidence: establish product structure, test bounded interaction, select a visual direction, then specify/build the authorized slice. A local experiment can deliberately defer appearance, but that deferral must name a follow-up before its styling becomes the implementation baseline. Reuse accepted behavior through that follow-up rather than rebuilding the flow to make it prettier.

D-01B is complete as an owner-reviewed M0 interaction experiment with qualified overall usability acceptance. Retain the unproven dimensions explicitly: Q-002 authorization policy, Q-005 review-evidence sufficiency, individual exceptional-state comprehension and accessibility beyond the limited checks. Q-004 is partially informed, not fully resolved. Add D-01E for visual direction and representative state specification before B-01 readiness; it is design work, not implementation authorization. Resume the already queued R-05 feasibility work next, then R-04A. Status remains the routing authority.

## Evaluate the process on the next application

Record whether an unaccepted surrounding decision is discovered before construction; whether owner review repeats a known structural defect; which quality dimensions are accepted or rejected; and what rework follows. Record elapsed effort only when actually measured. A future unrelated small task should also demonstrate that this procedure stays proportional. Do not create a new mandatory report per task: a short work-record paragraph can supply these observations.

Process outcome: one live sequence now has owner-reported improvement in usability after applying the revised process. General effectiveness, efficiency and independent replication remain unproven. Task outcome: a usable interaction baseline, rejected appearance, and an explicit visual-design follow-up rather than silent promotion of prototype CSS.
